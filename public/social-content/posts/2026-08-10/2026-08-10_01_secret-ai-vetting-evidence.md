### English

Government participation is not a safety certificate when the review process is voluntary and its criteria are not public. Axios reported on 3 August that the White House had finalized an advanced-model vetting framework, while its contents, scope and implementation timeline remained undisclosed. The Guardian reported on 7 August that testing criteria would be shared only with selected companies. In parallel, the UK AI minister said on 3 August that regulation remained an option if voluntary safeguards failed.

Enterprise buyers therefore need their own evidence contract: a comparable model card, clearly scoped cyber evaluations, material-incident notification, known limitations, and retesting after meaningful model or system changes. This is not a demand for every sensitive benchmark detail; it is a requirement for enough verifiable evidence to make an accountable deployment decision.

Can your AI supplier provide evidence that your risk committee can assess independently?

### English

**Title: When Government AI Tests Stay Private: Build an Enterprise Evidence Gate**

- Separate “government testing engagement” from a verifiable safety assurance.
- Require suppliers to disclose evaluation scope, version, limitations, environment and risks not covered.
- Put material AI-security incident notification and investigation support into contracts.
- Trigger reassessment after major model, tool or connector changes.
- Require Security, Legal, Procurement and the business owner to sign off residual risk.
