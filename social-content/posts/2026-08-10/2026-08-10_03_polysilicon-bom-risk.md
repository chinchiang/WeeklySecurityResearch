### English

Semiconductor supply-chain risk does not begin at fab capacity. It can begin with a policy change affecting an upstream material. Reuters reported on 6 August that the United States set minimum import prices and a 15% tariff on polysilicon and derivative products, with implementation scheduled for 4 December. The action spans both solar and semiconductor supply chains.

For electronics manufacturers, “prices may rise” is not a sufficient assessment. Put supplier country, contract repricing clauses, wafer lead times, energy-project exposure and inventory coverage on one scenario map. Then test alternate-material and delivery assumptions before the effective date. The policy may support domestic capacity, but a delayed start also creates the possibility of pre-effective-date import movement and uneven pass-through across tiers.

Can your BOM risk review trace policy exposure beyond the tier-one supplier to upstream materials?
